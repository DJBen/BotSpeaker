Decision Digest turns a completed video meeting into a structured summary containing decisions, action items, open questions, and links back to transcript timestamps. The product is aimed first at project teams that already record internal planning meetings but lose time reconstructing commitments afterward. Twenty-four design partners have used the pilot for eight weeks, representing roughly eighty-four hundred eligible employees and about thirty-two thousand processed meetings. Activation is strong when a workspace administrator enables the feature and explains the consent model. Weekly retention among people who opened at least three digests is sixty-eight percent, and those users report saving an average of fourteen minutes per meeting. The value is real, but those headline numbers do not by themselves establish launch readiness.

The quality picture is encouraging and uneven. Human reviewers rated eighty-nine percent of decision statements as fully supported by the transcript and another seven percent as directionally correct but missing context. Action-item extraction reached ninety-two percent precision and eighty-one percent recall. The misses cluster in meetings with rapid speaker changes, indirect commitments, or mixed languages. In the pilot, two summaries assigned an action to the wrong person and one converted a tentative proposal into a final decision. No customer acted on those errors before correcting the digest, but these are exactly the mistakes that can damage trust. For general availability, the digest must remain visibly editable, every statement must link to source context, and the interface must describe the output as a draft rather than an authoritative record.

Our proposed launch scope is intentionally narrower than the full vision. General availability will support scheduled internal meetings in English, Spanish, French, and German. It will require an organization administrator to enable the capability, a meeting organizer to turn it on, and an in-meeting notice visible to every attendee. External meetings, webinars, regulated-workflow templates, automatic task creation, and coaching scores remain excluded. Digests will be generated only after the meeting ends, and nothing will be sent automatically to task systems or email lists. Organizers can review, edit, or delete a digest before sharing. This boundary preserves the core time-saving experience while avoiding irreversible downstream actions during the first release.

I want to separate launch gates from follow-up improvements. A launch gate protects a promise that appears in the product or our customer agreement. Before release, we need transcript-grounding links on every generated item, reliable attendee notice, deletion propagation within the documented window, workspace-level disable controls, and rollback that stops new processing without losing original meeting data. We also need the ninety-fifth-percentile digest time below fifteen seconds for a sixty-minute meeting, because the product experience implies the result is available immediately after the call. Custom summary styles, more languages, mobile editing, and direct integrations are valuable, but they are not required to safely deliver the initial promise.

The rollout plan is a staged availability process, not a single global switch. In week one, we will invite five existing design partners that have completed the revised administrator checklist. The feature flag can disable new digest generation by organization, region, or meeting type. Success will be measured as a balanced scorecard. Adoption means at least thirty-five percent of eligible meetings produce a digest and at least half of those digests are opened. Utility means thirty-day retained usage above fifty percent and a customer-reported time saving above ten minutes per used meeting. Trust means fewer than one substantiated unsupported-decision report per thousand digests, no notice-delivery failures, deletion completion above ninety-nine point nine percent within the stated window, and fewer than three support contacts per hundred enabled workspaces during the first month. Reliability means a successful generation rate above ninety-nine point five percent and ninety-fifth-percentile completion under fifteen seconds. We will not trade away trust metrics to improve adoption.
